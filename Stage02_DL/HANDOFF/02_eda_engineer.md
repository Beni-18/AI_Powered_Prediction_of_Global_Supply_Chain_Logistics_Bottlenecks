## Decisions and assumptions (what you decided and why, in plain English)
- Everything that steers a modelling or alert decision uses **train hours only** (rule choice, noise floor, lead-lag). Test origins are used only to look at attribution and attention, where nothing is chosen.
- Noise floor = MAD x 1.4826 of the past-only residual on train hours (not the standard deviation: dwell is spiky).
- Residual = dwell - train weekly profile - trailing 14-day median. Past hours only, so the rule can run live on the dashboard.
- Lead-lag scans **0-28 days**, not the 0-14 in the role file: 13 of the 50 lanes take longer than 14 days. Before correlating, each series is deseasonalised, scaled by its train IQR, the network-wide average is removed (shared drivers such as peak season would otherwise look like propagation) and smoothed over 24 h. Hourly prewhitening was tried first and found nothing; the lane effect lives in the slow level.
- "Chance" for lead-lag = unlinked node pairs given a random lane transit time (seeded); the test is binomial on hits within +-2 days (sim) / +-3 days (real).
- Attribution explains the **learnt change from the last value**: the models predict anchor + change, and the anchor is held fixed.
- The 5 ambiguous episodes: the longest labour strike, the strongest demand surge, a short high-severity equipment failure, and two spikes with no event at the node or a linked node. No chokepoint blockage happens in train/val, so none is used.
