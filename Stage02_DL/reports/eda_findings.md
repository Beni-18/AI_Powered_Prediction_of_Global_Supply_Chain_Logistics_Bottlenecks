# Stage 2 - EDA findings (time series, propagation, attribution)

Generated by `Stage02_DL/02_eda_engineer.py` (numbers inserted by code). Model artefacts: v2.0. Train hours for every modelling decision; test origins only for the attribution / attention checks, where nothing is chosen. Correlations are associations, not causes.

## Findings

### F1. Dwell has long memory; its daily and weekly cycles are weak

- **What:** Median decorrelation time of dwell is 80 h (5 of 27 nodes stay above 1/e beyond 168 h); median ACF 0.68 at 24 h, 0.44 at 72 h, 0.17 at 168 h. Daily bump at 0/27 nodes, weekly bump at 1/27. ADF rejects a unit root for 99% of 208 node x channel series. PACF: lag 1 = 0.89, lag 2 = 0.42.
- **Evidence:** `eda_acf_summary.csv`, `eda_acf.png`
- **So what:** Keep the 72 h window (it covers most of the memory) and keep seasonal-naive (t-168 h) as a baseline, expecting it to lose to persistence because the cycles are weak. Most of the memory is a slow level, so the 4-week context and the 'last value + learnt change' design are justified; a 168 h window is worth testing (Role 1 hparam study), not assuming.

### F2. The slow trend, not the daily or weekly cycle, carries most of the dwell variance

- **What:** MSTL on train hours, median over nodes: trend 53%, daily 2%, weekly 4%, residual 26%. Throughput: trend 30%, daily 7%, weekly 8%.
- **Evidence:** `eda_stl_components.csv`, `eda_stl_components.png`
- **So what:** Calendar features alone cannot forecast dwell; the model must track the level. A seasonal-adjusted residual (not raw dwell) is the right input for the shock / structural rule.

### F3. An explicit rule separates seasonal peaks, transient shocks and structural bottlenecks

- **What:** Rule: residual above 3 x noise floor for >= 24 h = structural. On the 5 ambiguous graphs it agrees with the ground truth on 5/5 (event -> flagged, no event -> not structural). Against all train + val events: structural rule recall 29% with 0.06 episodes per node per 30 days, 93% of them near an event; Role 4's p95 rule recall 71%, 0.41 episodes per node per 30 days, 62% near an event.
- **Evidence:** `eda_ambiguous_graphs.md`, `eda_ambiguous_1..5.png`, `eda_episode_rule_check.csv`
- **So what:** Role 4 decides whether to keep the p95 rule, switch, or report both (see recommendations).

### F4. Congestion travels along the lanes with roughly the lane's transit time (simulated data)

- **What:** Best lag within 2 days of the contract transit time on 36/62 lane directions (58%) vs 11% for 602 unlinked pairs (binomial p = 1e-19). When it hits, the data lag is +10 h from the contract (median). But the correlation itself is weak: only 10 lane directions beat the 95th percentile of unlinked pairs (r = 0.39); median r at the transit lag = 0.17.
- **Evidence:** `eda_leadlag_edges.csv`, `eda_leadlag_heatmap.png`
- **So what:** The transit-delayed lane messages in the graph model point at the right lag, so the design is sound; the weak strength is consistent with the negative graph ablation (little extra signal to gain). Propagation weights (Stage 5) can use the contract transit time; the data lag is +10 h from it.

### F5. On REAL PortWatch ship counts the lane lag signal is present

- **What:** Best lag within 3 days of the transit time on 23/49 lane directions (47%) vs 21% for unlinked pairs (p = 6.2e-05); 11 directions beat the unlinked 95th percentile (r = 0.39). Train years 2019-2023, daily, 7-day smoothing.
- **Evidence:** `eda_leadlag_edges_real.csv`, `eda_leadlag_real_heatmap.png`
- **So what:** This is the honest real-world check of the graph idea: say exactly this in the debate, and do not claim lane propagation on real data beyond what these numbers show.

### F6. Filled-in hours are few, short and spread out

- **What:** 2,379 filled-in hours (1.0% of all), longest gap 2 h, 3% of them in gaps of 2 h or more. Top 3 nodes hold 13% (chi-square vs even spread p = 0.48). Rate inside event windows 1.07% vs outside 1.00% (x1.07); above the alert line 0.77% vs 1.02%.
- **Evidence:** `eda_imputed_hours.csv`, `eda_imputed_hours.json`, `eda_imputed_hours.png`
- **So what:** Forward-fill up to 6 h (Role 3) is adequate; keep the is_imputed input flag and keep excluding imputed target hours from the metrics (Role 4).

### F7. The forecasters lean on their own recent history and the operational drivers; the reporting flag gets ~0

- **What:** LSTM dwell forecast (integrated gradients, 37 test origins): top channels avg_dwell_time_hours, capacity_utilization_pct, equipment_availability_pct; drivers 37%, own history 49%, yard state 11%, calendar 3%, is_imputed flag 0.0%. Last 6 h carry 82% and the last 24 h 94% of the attribution. Transformer: top channels avg_dwell_time_hours, equipment_availability_pct, capacity_utilization_pct, last 6 h 58%. Completeness gap (median) 0.3%.
- **Evidence:** `eda_attribution_channels.csv/png`, `eda_attribution_timesteps.csv/png`
- **So what:** Attribution covers the learnt change from the last value (the anchor is held fixed). Channels near zero are candidates to drop in a Role 1 ablation, not to drop blindly.

### F8. Noise-channel sanity test: PASS

- **What:** On the LSTM trained with two extra inputs, pure noise gets 0.77% and a time-shuffled copy of dwell 0.61% of the dwell attribution. Shuffling both in time changes the test pinball loss by -0.01%; shuffling a real driver (utilisation) changes it by +4.64%.
- **Evidence:** `eda_noise_channel_test.json`
- **So what:** The attribution method is not just reflecting input magnitudes: it ignores inputs that carry no signal. Use this as the evidence when someone asks whether the model looks at genuine signals.

### F9. Transformer attention is spread almost evenly over the 72 h window, so it explains little

- **What:** The latest hour sends 9% of its attention to the last 6 h (uniform would be 8%); 1-48 h before an episode: 10% (131 node-origins). A filled-in input hour gets x0.98 the attention of a measured hour (2103 windows with both).
- **Evidence:** `eda_attention_episodes.png`, `eda_attention_by_lag.csv`, `eda_attention_summary.json`
- **So what:** Attention weights are not an explanation of the forecast; use the integrated-gradients numbers (F7) in the debate. Filled-in hours are neither ignored nor over-used by attention.

### F10. graph_full takes 23% of its dwell attribution from other nodes, 91% of that from lane neighbours

- **What:** graph_full: 23% of a node's dwell attribution comes from other nodes, 21% from lane neighbours (median lift vs an even spread x7.3). graph_none (sanity): 0.00% from other nodes.
- **Evidence:** `eda_graph_attribution.csv`, `eda_graph_attribution.png`
- **So what:** The graph path is really used and it follows the lanes (graph_none confirms the method gives 0 when no lanes exist). Read together with Role 4's ablation (no accuracy gain), this is consistent with the neighbours' information being largely redundant with a node's own history (F4: weak lane correlation). More graph capacity is unlikely to pay off on this data.

## Recommendations

**Role 1 (ML)**
- Keep the 72 h window as default; test 168 h in the hparam study (dwell memory: median decorrelation 80 h). Keep the 1/6/24/72/168 h horizons and the seasonal-naive baseline.
- Keep node embeddings: memory and variance shares differ by node type (`eda_acf_summary.csv`, `eda_stl_components.csv`).
- Keep the transit-delayed messages at the contract transit time; the data lag runs +10 h from it (median where it matches), well inside one daily context block, so no change is needed (`eda_leadlag_edges.csv`, column `lag_error_h`). Do not enlarge the graph part.
- Use the channel attribution (`eda_attribution_channels.csv`) to pick candidates for a drop-one-channel ablation on val.

**Role 3 (Data & Integration)**
- Forward-fill <= 6 h is adequate (`eda_imputed_hours.json`); keep `is_imputed` in the grid and in the API inputs.
- The dashboard can show the structural / transient / seasonal label from the rule in `eda_ambiguous_graphs.md` (past hours only, so it runs live).

**Role 4 (Evaluation)**
- Alert definition: the p95 rule catches 71% of events with 0.41 episodes per node per 30 days; the structural rule catches 29% with 0.06. Report lead time under both, choose on val, never on test (`eda_episode_rule_check.csv`).
- Quote the noise-channel result and the real-data lead-lag numbers exactly as above; neither is a claim of causation.
